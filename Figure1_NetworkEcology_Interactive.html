Reading this figure: Nodes represent actors in a regional media information ecology. Node size encodes eigenvector centrality — an actor's cascade potential derived from connection to other high-influence nodes, the primary structural mechanism behind credibility laundering. Node color encodes Centrality Risk tier computed from the composite formula: Centrality Risk = 0.25(Degree) + 0.35(Betweenness) + 0.20(Closeness) + 0.20(Eigenvector). Directed edges show observed information transfers; edge thickness reflects amplification weight. The post-intervention state models a containment-only intervention — tie-strength reduction on high-risk nodes without actor removal — consistent with the epidemiological principle of reducing transmission virulence while preserving network integrity. Closeness centrality is purely topological and does not change under tie-strength reduction alone, which is why Community E's Critical tier persists across both states (see Section 8).

Risk tiers represent relative structural cascade potential within this network snapshot — not claims about actor intent, content accuracy, or political orientation. The framework is content-neutral and jurisdictionally agnostic.
