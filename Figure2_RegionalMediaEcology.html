Reading this figure: This is a hypothetical regional media ecology constructed for illustrative purposes. All actor names are generic. The map demonstrates what a completed Network Ecology of Information analysis produces at regional scale — showing the full distribution of structural roles across a realistic mix of actor types. Node size encodes eigenvector centrality: the larger the node, the greater its cascade amplification potential through connection to other influential actors. Edge direction shows the flow of observed information transfers (repost, citation, hyperlink, mention); edge thickness reflects amplification weight on the 1–4 scale (mention=1, hyperlink=4). Use the actor-type filters to isolate transmission pathways by category — this reveals, for example, that automated accounts (bots) serve as secondary amplifiers feeding into community clusters rather than originating cascades independently.

Risk tiers represent relative structural cascade potential within this network snapshot — not claims about actor intent, content accuracy, or political orientation. The framework is content-neutral and jurisdictionally agnostic.
