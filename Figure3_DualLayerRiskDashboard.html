Dual-layer risk dashboard: structural tier × content verification status
Two independent analytical layers: structural screening score from network structure (content-neutral) and content status assigned by journalist observation. Together they produce an intervention priority — never by combining scores, but by reading their intersection.
Core principle: High amplification of verified content is a positive network signal — the ecology is working correctly. High amplification of unverified or contested content through the same structurally powerful node is the cascade risk that demands intervention. The two layers must remain analytically separate: structural prominence is mathematical; content status is observational. Neither makes a claim about truth — only about verification status and transmission capacity.

Reading this figure: Structural prominence is the structural screening score of each actor in the 18-actor network of Figure 2, computed as 0.25×Degree + 0.35×Betweenness + 0.20×Closeness + 0.20×Eigenvector (each max-normalised within the network) and assigned to a tier: Critical (≥ 0.85), High (≥ 0.65), Medium (≥ 0.35) or Low. Content status is assigned by journalist observation (illustrative values). Intervention priority is read from the intersection of the two layers as defined in Tables 5 and 6 of the paper; the layers are never combined into a single score.

Structural prominence scores represent network position only, not assessments of actor intent, content accuracy, or political orientation. Content status categories describe verification status as observed by journalists, not truth claims.
