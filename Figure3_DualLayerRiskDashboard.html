Dual-layer risk dashboard — structural amplification capacity × content verification status
Two independent analytical layers. Column 1: structural risk derived from network centrality metrics (content-neutral). Column 2: content status assigned by journalist observation. Together they produce an intervention priority — never by combining scores, but by reading their intersection.
Core principle: High amplification of verified content is a positive network signal — the ecology is working correctly. High amplification of unverified or contested content through the same structurally powerful node is the cascade risk that demands intervention. The two layers must remain analytically separate: structural risk is mathematical; content status is observational. Neither makes a claim about truth — only about verification status and transmission capacity.

Reading this figure: Structural risk (column 3) is derived entirely from network centrality metrics — weighted degree, betweenness, closeness, and eigenvector centrality (Centrality Risk = 0.25×Degree + 0.35×Betweenness + 0.20×Closeness + 0.20×Eigenvector) — and reflects an actor's capacity to amplify and transmit narratives at scale, independent of content. Content status (column 4) is assigned by journalist observation using five epistemic categories: Verified (independently confirmed by two or more sources), Contested (active factual dispute among credible sources), Unverified (single source, no independent confirmation), Satire (clearly labeled opinion or satirical content), and Procedural (official statements and institutional communications). These categories describe verification status, not political orientation or content quality.

Intervention priority (column 5) is determined by the intersection of both layers — never by combining them into a single score. A high structural risk actor amplifying verified content (Positive signal) requires no intervention; the network is functioning correctly. The same actor amplifying unverified content (Red alert) requires immediate containment. This distinction is the core applied contribution of the framework: amplification pathway alone is insufficient for intervention decisions — content verification status must be read alongside structural position.

Structural risk scores represent network position only — not assessments of actor intent, content accuracy, or political orientation. Content status categories describe verification status as observed by journalists — not truth claims. The framework is content-neutral in its structural layer and epistemically grounded in its content layer.
